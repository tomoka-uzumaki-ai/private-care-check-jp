Why the bank payout is smaller than reported sales

Separate refunds, platform fees, payout fees, unpaid amounts and confirmed paid costs before calling money profit.

Work through a complete fictional example

One JPY path reports gross sales of10,000, refunds of1,000 and platform fees of1,800: earned income before payout fees is7,200. A second path reports net income of3,000, so its refunds and platform fees are not deducted again. Together, earned income is10,200. Actual cash received for that same period is7,000 and fees withheld at receipt are200. The calculated unsettled amount is3,000, not3,200.

If all paid costs for that period are confirmed at2,000, received cash minus those costs is5,000. Payout fees are already reflected in the cash received and are not deducted twice. Unpaid costs, labor and taxes are outside this cash-result figure. These are invented examples, not our sales or verified deposits.

Check your own files in the right order

  1. Use the same period for reported income and its receipt. Separate deposits from other periods.
  2. Identify whether each reported amount is gross or already net. Unknown is not zero.
  3. Map columns explicitly; do not assume arbitrary platform exports already fit.
  4. Validate prepared files for exact headers, duplicates, period and cost rows.
  5. Only mark costs complete if all costs for that target are known. Keep JPY and USD separate.
